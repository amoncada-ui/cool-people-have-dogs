<p>This is about as low-effort as DIY dog enrichment gets — a muffin tin, some tennis balls, and treats turn into a genuinely engaging puzzle in under five minutes.</p>
<p><strong>Quick answer:</strong> drop treats into the wells of a muffin tin, cover each with a tennis ball, and let your dog figure out they need to move the balls to get the treats — that's the entire toy.</p>
<h2 id="muffin-tin" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">The Muffin Tin Version</h2>
<p>Drop a treat into each cup of a standard muffin tin, then cover each one with a tennis ball (or any ball that fits snugly). Your dog has to nose or paw the balls out one at a time to get to the treats underneath.</p>
<h2 id="towel-version" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">The Towel Roll-Up</h2>
<p>Scatter treats across a towel, then roll or fold it up so the treats are trapped inside the folds. Your dog has to unroll and manipulate the fabric to find them — a great low-cost option using something you already own.</p>
<h2 id="bottle-version" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">The Bottle Puzzle</h2>
<p>Cut a few holes slightly larger than your dog's kibble into an empty plastic bottle (remove the cap and label first), fill it with kibble, and let your dog roll it around to shake pieces out. Supervise to make sure they're not chewing off small plastic pieces.</p>
<h2 id="difficulty" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">Adjusting the Difficulty</h2>
<p>For a dog new to puzzle toys, use fewer hiding spots or looser folds to build confidence before making versions more challenging with more wells or tighter towel rolls.</p>
<h2 id="safety" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">Supervision &amp; Safety</h2>
<p>These toys are meant for supervised play — a determined chewer could damage a plastic bottle or swallow a tennis ball's fuzz over time, so keep an eye on things and retire any worn pieces.</p>
<h2 id="faq" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>How long will my dog stay engaged with this?</strong><br/>It varies by dog, but many spend 5-15 minutes working through a well-hidden version — it's a great activity to set up before you need a few quiet minutes.</p>
<p><strong>Can I use kibble instead of treats?</strong><br/>Absolutely — turning a portion of a meal into a puzzle activity is a great way to add enrichment without extra calories.</p>
<p><strong>My dog gets frustrated instead of engaged. What should I do?</strong><br/>Start easier than feels necessary — a loosely rolled towel or a muffin tin with treats barely covered builds the skill of "working" for food before ramping up difficulty.</p>
