faq:
- question: How much exercise does a senior dog need?
  answer: It varies by breed and health, but most senior dogs still benefit from daily movement — just shorter and gentler than in their younger years. Let your dog's energy and comfort guide the pace.
- question: My senior dog seems stiff in the mornings. Is that normal?
  answer: Some morning stiffness is common with age, but if it's severe, worsening, or paired with limping, it's worth a vet visit to rule out arthritis or another treatable condition.
- question: Should I change their food as they age?
  answer: Many senior dogs do well on a formula designed for lower activity levels and joint support, but any diet change is worth discussing with your vet first.

<p>An older dog doesn't need less of a routine — they need a different one. Shorter, more frequent activity, predictable timing, and a bit more patience go a long way toward keeping a senior dog comfortable and confident.</p>
<p><strong>Quick answer:</strong> the goal shifts from burning energy to managing comfort — shorter, more frequent walks, consistent meal and bathroom timing, and low-impact mental enrichment spread evenly through the day.</p>
<h2 id="morning" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">Morning: Ease Into It</h2>
<p>Senior joints are often stiffest first thing, so give your dog a few minutes to stretch and move around before a walk rather than heading straight out the door. A shorter, slower morning walk beats one long walk for most older dogs.</p>
<h2 id="midday" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">Midday: A Second, Shorter Walk</h2>
<p>Splitting exercise into two or three short walks instead of one long one reduces strain on aging joints while still meeting their activity needs. This is also a good window for a gentle <a class="cphd-inlinelink" href="/senior-games/">low-impact game</a> or a sniff-focused stroll.</p>
<h2 id="meals" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">Meals &amp; Medication</h2>
<p>Keep meal times consistent — senior dogs often do better with two smaller meals than one large one, and consistent timing helps if they're on any medication that needs to be given with food.</p>
<h2 id="evening" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">Evening: Comfort First</h2>
<p>A supportive orthopedic bed matters more with age, especially for dogs with arthritis. A calm wind-down routine (a short walk, then settling in for the night) helps signal that it's time to rest.</p>
<h2 id="monitoring" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">Watch for Changes</h2>
<p>Because routines with senior dogs are partly about monitoring, notice any changes in appetite, mobility, or bathroom habits — small shifts are often the first sign something's worth mentioning at their next vet visit.</p>
<h2 id="faq" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>How much exercise does a senior dog need?</strong><br/>It varies by breed and health, but most senior dogs still benefit from daily movement — just shorter and gentler than in their younger years. Let your dog's energy and comfort guide the pace.</p>
<p><strong>My senior dog seems stiff in the mornings. Is that normal?</strong><br/>Some morning stiffness is common with age, but if it's severe, worsening, or paired with limping, it's worth a vet visit to rule out arthritis or another treatable condition.</p>
<p><strong>Should I change their food as they age?</strong><br/>Many senior dogs do well on a formula designed for lower activity levels and joint support, but any diet change is worth discussing with your vet first.</p>
<p style="margin-top: 32px;">Consistency matters even more for senior dogs — a familiar routine reduces the anxiety that can come with aging senses and mobility changes.
