<p>A bandana is the easiest DIY dog accessory there is — no sewing machine required, just fabric, scissors, and about 15 minutes.</p>
<p><strong>Quick answer:</strong> cut a square of fabric, fold it into a triangle, hem or pink the edges to prevent fraying, and tie it around the collar or neck — that's the entire project.</p>
<h2 id="materials" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">What You'll Need</h2>
<p>A fat quarter of cotton fabric (roughly 18x22 inches works for most dogs), fabric scissors or pinking shears, and optionally fabric glue or a needle and thread if you want hemmed edges instead of raw ones.</p>
<h2 id="measure" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">1. Measure &amp; Cut</h2>
<p>Cut a square from your fabric — about 20x20 inches for a medium dog, smaller for a toy breed, larger for a big dog. Fold it diagonally into a triangle; this fold line becomes the part that sits along your dog's neck.</p>
<h2 id="edges" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">2. Finish the Edges</h2>
<p>Pinking shears give a clean zig-zag edge that resists fraying without any sewing. If you want a cleaner finish, a simple fold-and-glue or fold-and-stitch hem along the raw edges works well too.</p>
<h2 id="style" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">3. Add a Personal Touch</h2>
<p>Fabric paint, iron-on letters, or a small patch can personalize the bandana with your dog's name or a fun pattern. Let any paint fully dry and cure before your dog wears it.</p>
<h2 id="tie" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">4. Tie It On</h2>
<p>The classic over-the-collar tie (folded triangle draped over the collar and knotted at the sides) is the safest option since it releases easily if snagged, unlike a bandana knotted directly around the neck.</p>
<h2 id="faq" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Is it safe to leave a bandana on my dog all day?</strong><br/>For supervised time it's generally fine, but for long unsupervised stretches, especially with a crate or other dogs around, it's safer to remove it to avoid snagging.</p>
<p><strong>What fabric works best?</strong><br/>Lightweight cotton is easiest to cut and tie and holds up well to washing — avoid anything stretchy, which can bunch awkwardly.</p>
<p><strong>How do I know what size to cut?</strong><br/>A good rule of thumb is roughly your dog's neck measurement plus 8-10 inches per side of the triangle — when in doubt, cut a bit larger and trim down.</p>
<p style="margin-top: 32px;">Once you've made one, a small stack of bandanas in different patterns is a fun way to switch things up for seasons or holidays.
