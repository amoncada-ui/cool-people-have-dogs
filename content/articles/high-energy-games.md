<p>Some dogs just have more gas in the tank than a daily walk can burn off — and an under-exercised high-energy dog usually turns that energy into something you'd rather they didn't (digging, chewing, zoomies at 9pm). These games are built to actually tire them out, not just occupy five minutes.</p>
<p><strong>Quick answer:</strong> flirt pole and fetch variations burn the most energy fastest, while tug and find-it games add mental fatigue on top of physical — combining both is what really takes the edge off a high-drive dog.</p>
<h2 id="flirt-pole" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">1. Flirt Pole</h2>
<p>A long pole with a rope and a lure on the end — basically a giant cat toy for dogs. Whip it in unpredictable directions and let your dog chase, jump, and pounce. Ten minutes of flirt pole can tire a high-drive dog out faster than a 30-minute walk, since it combines sprinting with sudden direction changes.</p>
<h2 id="fetch-variations" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">2. Fetch With a Twist</h2>
<p>Standard fetch is great, but adding a bounce (throwing so the ball bounces unpredictably) or two balls (throw the second while they're bringing back the first, keeping them in constant motion) ramps up the intensity without needing more space.</p>
<h2 id="tug" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">3. Structured Tug</h2>
<p>Tug is a full-body workout for a dog and works even in a small space. Add a "drop it" cue between rounds to build impulse control alongside the physical burn — this makes it mentally tiring too, not just physical.</p>
<h2 id="find-it" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">4. Find It / Hide and Seek</h2>
<p>Hide treats or toys around a room or yard and send your dog to find them, or hide yourself and call them to seek you out. Sniffing and problem-solving are surprisingly tiring — this is a great option when the weather's bad but you still need to wear them out.</p>
<h2 id="treadmill" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">5. Treadmill Training</h2>
<p>With proper introduction and supervision, a dog treadmill lets high-energy breeds get a real run in regardless of weather or how much yard space you have. Start slow and never leave your dog unattended on one.</p>
<h2 id="agility" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">6. Backyard Agility Course</h2>
<p>You don't need real equipment — a broomstick between two chairs for a jump, a kiddie pool for a "tunnel" entrance, cones for weaving. The novelty and problem-solving of a course tires dogs out faster than repetitive fetch alone.</p>
<h2 id="faq" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>How much exercise does a high-energy dog actually need?</strong><br/>It varies hugely by breed, but many working and herding breeds need 60-90+ minutes of real exercise a day, not just a walk around the block.</p>
<p><strong>Can too much exercise be a problem?</strong><br/>Yes — especially high-arousal games like fetch and flirt pole can build tolerance, meaning your dog needs more and more to feel satisfied. Mixing in mentally tiring activities like find-it helps balance this out.</p>
<p><strong>My dog still has energy after all this. What now?</strong><br/>Consider whether the games are being paired with enough mental work — a tired body with an unstimulated brain still looks for trouble. A <a class="cphd-inlinelink" href="/interactive-toys/">puzzle toy</a> after a high-energy session often finishes the job.</p>
<p style="margin-top: 32px;">Rotating these games keeps things fresh for your dog and keeps you from getting stuck doing the same 20-minute fetch session every day. Pair a couple of these with a solid daily routine — see our <a class="cphd-inlinelink" href="/9to5-routine/">9-5 routine guide</a> for how to fit it all in.</p>
