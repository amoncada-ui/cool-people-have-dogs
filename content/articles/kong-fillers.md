<p>The Kong is one of those rare toys that's actually worth the hype — it's not just a chew toy, it's a built-in "give my brain something to do" machine. But a Kong is only as good as what's inside it, and a lot of people are underusing theirs with a plain scoop of peanut butter. Here's how to actually get the most mileage out of one.</p>
<p><strong>Quick answer:</strong> for maximum stall time, layer wet and dry ingredients and freeze it. For a quick reward with no prep, a smear of plain pumpkin puree or a few pieces of kibble wedged in works in under a minute.</p>
<h2 id="freezer" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">Freezer-Friendly Fillers</h2>
<p>These are the combos that turn a 5-minute snack into a 30-minute project — freeze them overnight and you've got a go-to on hand whenever you need it.</p>
<ul style="margin: 14px 0; padding-left: 22px; line-height: 1.9;">
<li><strong>Peanut butter + banana</strong> — mash together, stuff, freeze. The classic for a reason.</li>
<li><strong>Plain pumpkin puree + a spoon of plain yogurt</strong> — light, and gentle on tummies.</li>
<li><strong>Wet dog food + a few pieces of kibble</strong> — mix and pack tight, freeze solid.</li>
<li><strong>Mashed sweet potato + a little broth</strong> — low-fat option that still freezes well.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="quick" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">Quick Fillers (No Freezing Needed)</h2>
<p>For when you need a distraction right now:</p>
<ul style="margin: 14px 0; padding-left: 22px; line-height: 1.9;">
<li><strong>A smear of peanut butter</strong> — fast, effective, a little messy.</li>
<li><strong>Plain pumpkin puree</strong> — quick to load, easy to clean up.</li>
<li><strong>Kibble wedged in tight</strong> — good for mealtime enrichment instead of a bowl.</li>
<li><strong>Small training treats packed in</strong> — great for a quick independent-play moment.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="layering" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">The Layering Method (Makes It Last Way Longer)</h2>
<p>This is the trick that actually changes the game: plug the small hole at the bottom with a bit of peanut butter first, then layer in kibble, a wet layer (yogurt or pumpkin), more kibble, and top it off with something extra sticky like peanut butter or mashed banana. Freeze it standing up in a cup so it holds its shape. Your dog has to work through layers instead of one lick to the finish line.</p>
<h2 id="avoid" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">Fillers to Skip</h2>
<p>A few things that seem harmless but aren't worth the risk:</p>
<ul style="margin: 14px 0; padding-left: 22px; line-height: 1.9;">
<li><strong>Anything with xylitol</strong> — check the label on peanut butter every time; xylitol is toxic to dogs.</li>
<li><strong>Chocolate, grapes, raisins, onions, garlic</strong> — all off-limits, even in small filler amounts.</li>
<li><strong>Too much cheese or fatty leftovers</strong> — can upset a sensitive stomach fast.</li>
<li><strong>Anything sticky and hard as a rock straight from the freezer</strong> — let it sit a minute so it's not tooth-breaking hard.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="faq" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>How often can I give my dog a stuffed Kong?</strong><br/>Daily is fine for most dogs as part of their regular food or treat allowance — just account for the calories, especially with peanut butter-heavy fillings.</p>
<p><strong>What size Kong should I get?</strong><br/>Kongs are sized by breed weight — check the sizing chart on the package. Too small and it's a choking risk; too big and your dog may lose interest.</p>
<p><strong>My dog gets the filling out in two minutes. How do I slow them down?</strong><br/>Freeze it, use the layering method above, and try packing the filling in tighter with a spoon so there's less air space to work with.</p>
