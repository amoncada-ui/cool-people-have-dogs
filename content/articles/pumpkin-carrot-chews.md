<p><strong>Quick answer:</strong> 3 ingredients, 6 steps, and most of the work is hands-off. Here's exactly how to make it.</p>
<h2 id="ingredients" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">Ingredients</h2>
<ul style="margin: 14px 0; padding-left: 22px; line-height: 1.9;">
<li>2-3 medium carrots</li>
<li>1/2 cup pure pumpkin puree (not pie filling)</li>
<li>Parchment paper</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="instructions" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">Instructions</h2>
<ol style="margin: 14px 0; padding-left: 22px; line-height: 1.9;">
<li>Preheat the oven to 250°F (or set your dehydrator to the jerky/meat setting).</li>
<li>Thinly slice the carrots lengthwise into even strips, about 1/8 inch thick.</li>
<li>If using pumpkin puree too, spread it thinly on a parchment-lined tray in a separate batch.</li>
<li>Lay the carrot strips flat on a lined baking sheet, not touching.</li>
<li>Bake for 2-3 hours, flipping halfway, until leathery and dried out but still a little flexible.</li>
<li>Let cool completely before storing in an airtight container.</li>
</ol>
<div style="background: #FBF3EC; border: 1px solid #E8C4A8; border-radius: 10px; padding: 16px 20px; margin: 24px 0; font-size: 15px; line-height: 1.6;">
<strong style="color: #8A4A2E;">Good to know:</strong> These take patience more than skill — low and slow is what gets that chewy jerky texture instead of a limp, soggy carrot.
        </div>
<h2 id="faq" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Can I use a dehydrator instead of the oven?</strong><br/>Yes — a dehydrator on the meat/jerky setting works even better and uses less energy for a multi-hour dry.</p>
<p><strong>How long do they keep?</strong><br/>About 2 weeks in an airtight container at room temperature, or a couple months in the freezer.</p>
