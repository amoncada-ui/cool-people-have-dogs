<p>A basic dog house is a very doable weekend build even without advanced woodworking skills — the main things to get right are sizing, weatherproofing, and keeping the entrance out of direct wind and rain.</p>
<p><strong>Quick answer:</strong> size the house so your dog can stand, turn around, and lie down comfortably (not much bigger, for warmth), raise it off the ground, angle the roof for runoff, and offset the entrance away from prevailing wind.</p>
<h2 id="sizing" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">Sizing It Right</h2>
<p>A house that's too large won't retain body heat well in cold weather — aim for just enough room to stand, turn around, and lie down. Measure your dog at their current size (or expected adult size for a puppy) before cutting any wood.</p>
<h2 id="materials" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">Materials &amp; Tools</h2>
<p>Exterior-grade plywood, 2x4s for the frame, exterior wood screws, and shingles or roofing material for the top are the core materials. A basic drill, saw, and level cover the tools needed.</p>
<h2 id="frame" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">1. Build the Base &amp; Frame</h2>
<p>Raise the floor a few inches off the ground on a simple frame to keep it dry and insulated from cold ground. Build the wall frame next, leaving a doorway opening sized appropriately for your dog.</p>
<h2 id="walls-roof" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">2. Attach Walls &amp; Roof</h2>
<p>Attach exterior plywood panels to the frame for walls, then build a sloped roof (a single-slope or simple peaked design both work) to shed rain and snow away from the entrance.</p>
<h2 id="weatherproof" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">3. Weatherproof It</h2>
<p>Exterior paint or sealant on all wood surfaces protects against moisture and rot. A flap or offset entrance (rather than a doorway facing straight into wind) keeps the interior noticeably warmer in bad weather.</p>
<h2 id="placement" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">4. Placement</h2>
<p>Position the house in a spot with some shade in summer and wind protection in winter, and make sure it's on ground that drains well rather than a spot that pools water after rain.</p>
<h2 id="faq" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Does my dog actually need an insulated dog house?</strong><br/>If your dog spends significant time outdoors in cold weather, insulation (rigid foam board between double walls) makes a real difference — for mild climates, a well-placed, well-sealed basic house is often enough.</p>
<p><strong>How do I know what size to build?</strong><br/>Measure your dog's length and height at the shoulder, then add a small margin — bigger isn't better here since a too-large house is harder to keep warm.</p>
<p><strong>What roofing material works best?</strong><br/>Asphalt shingles matching your home, or a simple metal roofing panel, both hold up well and are inexpensive additions from a hardware store.</p>
