<p>Nail trims have a reputation for being the worst part of dog grooming — usually because of one bad experience, not because the task itself is hard. The right tools and a calm approach make most of the dread disappear.</p>
<p><strong>Quick answer:</strong> you need clippers or a grinder sized for your dog, styptic powder in case you nick the quick, and treats for positive reinforcement — going slow matters more than any specific tool.</p>
<h2 id="tools" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">Clippers vs. Grinders</h2>
<p>Guillotine or scissor-style clippers are quick once you're confident, while a grinder files gradually and is often less intimidating for nervous dogs since there's no sudden clip sound. Either works — pick based on what your dog tolerates better.</p>
<h2 id="quick" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">Finding the Quick</h2>
<p>The quick is the blood vessel inside the nail — on light-colored nails you can usually see it as a pink line; on dark nails, trim in small increments and watch for a dark dot appearing in the center, which signals you're getting close.</p>
<h2 id="styptic" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">Styptic Powder Is Non-Negotiable</h2>
<p>Even experienced trimmers nick the quick occasionally. Keep styptic powder (or cornstarch in a pinch) on hand to stop bleeding quickly — it looks scarier than it is, and a calm response keeps your dog from associating trims with panic.</p>
<h2 id="technique" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">Technique: Little and Often</h2>
<p>Trim a small amount, pause, reward, repeat — rushing to finish all nails in one sitting is what turns trims into a fight. For nail-shy dogs, doing just one or two nails a day is a completely valid approach.</p>
<h2 id="positive" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">Making It Positive</h2>
<p>Handle paws regularly outside of trim time so nail day isn't the only time feet get touched. High-value treats during and after trims build a much better association than treats alone can undo after a bad experience.</p>
<h2 id="faq" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>How often should I trim my dog's nails?</strong><br/>Most dogs need a trim every 3-4 weeks, though active dogs on pavement may wear nails down naturally and need it less often. If you hear clicking on hard floors, it's time.</p>
<p><strong>My dog won't let me touch their paws. What now?</strong><br/>Start with just touching paws and rewarding, with no clippers involved, for several sessions before attempting an actual trim. Building tolerance gradually beats forcing it every time.</p>
<p><strong>What if I cut the quick?</strong><br/>Apply styptic powder (or press the nail into cornstarch) and hold light pressure for a minute — it will stop bleeding and looks worse than it is. Stay calm so your dog doesn't pick up on your stress.</p>
