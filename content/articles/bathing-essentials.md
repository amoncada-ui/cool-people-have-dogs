<p>Half the battle of bath time is having everything within reach before your dog gets in the tub — nothing derails a calm bath faster than dripping across the bathroom for a forgotten towel. Here's what to gather first.</p>
<p><strong>Quick answer:</strong> dog-specific shampoo, a non-slip mat, a cup or sprayer, and 2-3 absorbent towels cover the basics. Treats and a brush make the whole thing go smoother.</p>
<h2 id="essentials" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">The Core Essentials</h2>
<p>Dog-specific shampoo (human shampoo can dry out or irritate their skin), a non-slip mat for the tub floor (a huge confidence booster for dogs who hate the slippery feeling), a cup or handheld sprayer for controlled rinsing, and 2-3 absorbent towels — dogs hold a lot more water than you'd expect.</p>
<h2 id="before" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">Before They Get In</h2>
<p>A quick brush-out removes loose fur and prevents matting once they're wet (wet fur mats much faster than dry). Have high-value treats ready nearby to reward calm behavior throughout, not just at the end.</p>
<h2 id="during" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">During the Bath</h2>
<p>Lukewarm water (test it on your wrist first), working shampoo in with your hands starting at the neck and moving back, and keeping water and suds away from the eyes and inside the ears. A cotton ball loosely placed in each ear can help keep water out.</p>
<h2 id="drying" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">Drying</h2>
<p>A microfiber towel absorbs more water than a regular bath towel and speeds things up considerably. For dogs who tolerate it, a pet-specific dryer on a low, cool setting finishes the job faster than air-drying — never use a human hair dryer's hot setting, which can overheat sensitive skin.</p>
<h2 id="optional" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">Nice-to-Haves</h2>
<p>A detangling spray for longer coats, ear-cleaning solution to use after the bath, and paw balm if baths tend to dry out their pads. None of these are essential for a basic bath, but they round things out nicely for dogs who bathe regularly.</p>
<h2 id="faq" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>How often should I bathe my dog?</strong><br/>Most dogs do well with a bath every 4-6 weeks, though it varies by coat type, activity level, and skin condition — too-frequent bathing can actually dry out their skin.</p>
<p><strong>Can I use human shampoo in a pinch?</strong><br/>Better to avoid it — a dog's skin has a different pH than ours, and human shampoo can cause dryness or irritation. If you're ever truly stuck, a baby shampoo is the gentlest substitute.</p>
<p><strong>My dog hates baths. What helps?</strong><br/>A non-slip mat and lukewarm (not cold) water make a big difference, plus going slowly and pairing the whole process with treats. Some dogs also do better with a handheld sprayer versus being submerged.</p>
<p style="margin-top: 32px;">Once you've got a routine down, bath time gets easier every time — dogs pick up on the pattern just like they do with any other routine.
