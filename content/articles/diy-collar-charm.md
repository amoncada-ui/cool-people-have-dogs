<p>A simple charm is a fun, low-cost way to make your dog's collar a little more "them" — and this version needs no tools, no jewelry-making experience, and about 20 minutes.</p>
<p><strong>Quick answer:</strong> thread beads onto durable cord, secure both ends, and attach it to a collar D-ring with a small jump ring or split ring — that's the whole project.</p>
<h2 id="materials" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">What You'll Need</h2>
<p>A selection of beads (wood, silicone, or acrylic all work — avoid anything with sharp edges or small enough to be a choking hazard), a length of paracord or waxed cord, a small metal split ring or jump ring, and scissors. A lighter helps seal the cord ends if you're using nylon cord.</p>
<h2 id="pick-beads" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">1. Pick Your Beads &amp; Plan the Pattern</h2>
<p>Lay your beads out in the order you want before threading anything — it's much easier to swap colors around now than after they're strung. A simple color pattern (like alternating two colors, or a name spelled in letter beads) reads best from a distance.</p>
<h2 id="thread" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">2. Thread the Cord</h2>
<p>Cut a length of cord about twice as long as you want the finished charm plus a few extra inches for tying. Thread your beads on in your planned order, leaving enough bare cord on each end to tie off securely.</p>
<h2 id="secure" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">3. Secure Both Ends</h2>
<p>Tie a tight double or triple knot at each end right against the last bead so nothing slides off. If using nylon cord, carefully melt the very tip of each end with a lighter for a couple seconds to prevent fraying — let it cool before handling.</p>
<h2 id="attach" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">4. Attach to the Collar</h2>
<p>Loop the finished charm through a small split ring, then clip or thread that ring onto your dog's collar D-ring (the same ring their ID tag usually hangs from). Give it a firm tug test before letting your dog wear it unsupervised.</p>
<h2 id="faq" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Is this safe for a dog who chews on their collar?</strong><br/>Small dangling charms aren't a great fit for dogs who mouth or chew at their own gear — save this one for dogs who leave their collar alone, and always supervise a new accessory at first.</p>
<p><strong>What beads should I avoid?</strong><br/>Skip anything small enough to be swallowed, glass beads that could shatter, or anything with sharp edges. Larger wood or silicone beads are the safest bet.</p>
<p><strong>How long does a charm like this last?</strong><br/>With normal wear it should hold up for months, but check the knots periodically and replace the cord if it starts looking frayed.</p>
<p style="margin-top: 32px;">Once you've got the basic technique down, it's easy to make a few in different color combos to match different leashes or the season.
