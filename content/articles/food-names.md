<p>I'll be honest — I'm a little biased toward food names. There's something about yelling "MEATBALL, DROP IT" across a park that just hits different. Food names tend to be warm, a little silly, and instantly likable, which is basically the whole vibe you want in a dog name anyway. Here's the full spread, sorted by what's actually in your kitchen.</p>
<p><strong>Quick picks, if you want to skip ahead:</strong> Mango, Biscuit, Waffle, Peaches, Olive, and Pretzel are the six I see getting the most love lately.</p>
<h2 id="fruits" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">Fruit Names</h2>
<p>Sweet, bright, and usually pretty easy to yell across a backyard.</p>
<ul style="margin: 14px 0; padding-left: 22px; line-height: 1.9;">
<li><strong>Mango</strong> — tropical, upbeat, works for almost any coat color.</li>
<li><strong>Peaches</strong> — soft and Southern-sounding, great for a sweetheart of a dog.</li>
<li><strong>Berry</strong> — small, snappy, and cute on a tiny dog or a giant one ironically.</li>
<li><strong>Kiwi</strong> — a little quirky, fun to say, great for a scrappy little dog.</li>
<li><strong>Plum</strong> — short, a little fancy, ages well.</li>
<li><strong>Clementine</strong> — a mouthful, but a genuinely great name for a dog with a lot of personality.</li>
<li><strong>Melon</strong> — goofy in the best way, fits a big, floppy dog.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="veggies" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">Vegetable Names</h2>
<p>Underrated category, honestly — these names tend to feel down-to-earth and a little funny.</p>
<ul style="margin: 14px 0; padding-left: 22px; line-height: 1.9;">
<li><strong>Basil</strong> — technically an herb, but it's earned its spot — classy and easy to say.</li>
<li><strong>Bean</strong> — one of the most popular small-dog names out there for good reason.</li>
<li><strong>Pepper</strong> — spunky, great for a dog with a lot of energy.</li>
<li><strong>Olive</strong> — timeless, a little sophisticated, works on any size dog.</li>
<li><strong>Sprout</strong> — perfect for a puppy or a small breed that stays little forever.</li>
<li><strong>Yam</strong> — silly and lovable, great for a goofball.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="savory" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">Savory &amp; Comfort Food Names</h2>
<p>For the dog who's basically a big warm hug shaped like a meal:</p>
<ul style="margin: 14px 0; padding-left: 22px; line-height: 1.9;">
<li><strong>Meatball</strong> — round, warm, and honestly the best name for a stocky little dog.</li>
<li><strong>Waffle</strong> — cheerful, a little goofy, hard not to smile at.</li>
<li><strong>Biscuit</strong> — the reigning champion of food names for a reason.</li>
<li><strong>Nacho</strong> — playful and a little chaotic, matches a certain kind of dog perfectly.</li>
<li><strong>Bagel</strong> — round, friendly, great for a dog who's always underfoot in the kitchen.</li>
<li><strong>Taco</strong> — short, fun to yell, universally loved.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="faq" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>What's the most popular food-inspired dog name?</strong><br/>Biscuit and Bean are two of the most consistently popular food names out there — familiar, warm, and easy to call.</p>
<p><strong>Are food names better for small dogs or big dogs?</strong><br/>Honestly, either. Sweet names like Peaches or Berry tend to suit smaller dogs, while something like Meatball or Waffle can be genuinely funny on a huge dog. There's no wrong answer.</p>
<p><strong>Should I avoid naming my dog after food I eat a lot?</strong><br/>Not really a dealbreaker — most people find it more charming than confusing. The only real snag is if you're constantly cooking with the ingredient and your dog thinks every mention is about them (which, to be fair, might just make dinner more fun).</p>
