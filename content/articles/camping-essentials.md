<p>Camping with your dog can be one of the best parts of a trip — or a stressful mess if you forget something basic three hours from the nearest store. A little planning goes a long way outdoors.</p>
<p><strong>Quick answer:</strong> pack a long line, a portable water bowl, a copy of vaccination records, flea/tick prevention, a first-aid kit, and a way to secure them at the site — those cover almost every camping scenario.</p>
<h2 id="containment" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">Containment at the Campsite</h2>
<p>A long line (15-30 feet) clipped to a stake lets your dog roam safely without wandering into another site or the treeline. A tie-out with a swivel prevents tangling, and a properly fitted collar or harness is a must in case they slip the line.</p>
<h2 id="sleeping" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">Sleeping Setup</h2>
<p>Bring their regular bed or a packable camping mat — familiar bedding helps them settle in an unfamiliar place, and it keeps them off cold or wet ground overnight. A blanket for the tent doubles as extra warmth if temperatures drop.</p>
<h2 id="food-water" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">Food &amp; Water</h2>
<p>Pack more food than you think you'll need, plus a collapsible bowl for both food and water. A gallon or two of extra water is worth carrying even at sites with a water source, since dogs drink more with extra activity.</p>
<h2 id="health-safety" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">Health &amp; Safety Kit</h2>
<p>Flea and tick prevention is non-negotiable for wooded areas, and a basic pet first-aid kit (gauze, vet wrap, tweezers for ticks or splinters) covers most minor mishaps. Bring a copy of vaccination records, especially if the campground requires proof.</p>
<h2 id="etiquette" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">Campground Etiquette</h2>
<p>Check the site's leash rules before you go — many require dogs on-leash at all times, even at your own site. Pack extra waste bags and always clean up, since a few bad experiences with dogs can get pet policies revoked for everyone.</p>
<h2 id="faq" style="font-family: 'Fredoka', sans-serif; font-weight: 600; font-size: 26px; margin: 36px 0 14px;">Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Can I let my dog off-leash at a campsite?</strong><br/>Only where explicitly allowed and only if your dog has rock-solid recall — most campgrounds require leashes at all times, and wildlife encounters are a real risk off-leash.</p>
<p><strong>How do I keep my dog warm overnight?</strong><br/>A packable dog sleeping bag or extra blankets inside the tent work well; for cold-weather trips, a dog with a thin coat may need a jacket even inside a tent.</p>
<p><strong>What if my dog isn't used to sleeping outside?</strong><br/>A backyard trial run with the tent and gear before the real trip helps enormously — it turns the first real night away from a totally novel experience into a familiar one.</p>
